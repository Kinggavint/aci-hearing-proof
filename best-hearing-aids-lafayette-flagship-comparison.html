The short version

There is no single best hearing aid. There is only the best hearing aid for your listening life. That is the honest answer from the fitting room.

At ACI Hearing Center we fit five flagship prescription hearing aid platforms, one from each of the major global manufacturers. Every one of them is a legitimate best pick for some patients and the wrong pick for others. This page is the cheat sheet.

  • Signia Integrated Xperience is our top pick for group conversation, own-voice quality, and longest battery life.
  • Oticon Real is our top pick for fully hands-off natural listening across environments.
  • Phonak Audeo Sphere Infinio is our top pick for Roger remote-microphone situations and high-computation AI in noise.
  • ReSound Nexia is our top pick for Auracast breadth today at Lafayette public venues.
  • Starkey Genesis AI is our top pick for on-board fall detection with texted alerts, Live Language Translation, and the highest rechargeable power ceiling.

Who is each flagship best for?

Signia Integrated Xperience

Best for: patients whose hardest listening life is group conversation at restaurants, family dinners, worship services, or meetings. Signia\'s RealTime Conversation Enhancement (RTCE) tracks up to four voices at once and shifts focus to whoever is talking, which is a real step up from single-target beamforming. Own Voice Processing is the best-in-class solution for patients who quit hearing aids in the past because their own voice sounded "in a barrel." The 28-hour rechargeable RIC battery is the longest in the flagship tier.

Not the top pick for: patients who want fully hands-off natural sound (Oticon Real is), patients who need Roger remote-mic (Phonak is), or patients who want on-board fall detection (Starkey is).

Read the full audiologist review of Signia Integrated Xperience.

Oticon Real

Best for: patients who want to put in hearing aids and forget about them. Oticon\'s BrainHearing philosophy and Deep Neural Network processing deliver an open, natural sound scene without asking the wearer to switch programs or make manual adjustments. Excellent for patients coming from years of untreated loss who need the brain to relearn a full sound environment gradually. Well tolerated by first-time users.

Not the top pick for: patients whose main struggle is a very noisy restaurant table where they need aggressive beamforming (Signia or Phonak win), or patients who want a device that responds to voice commands or has an in-app AI helper.

Read the full audiologist review of Oticon Real.

Phonak Audeo Sphere Infinio

Best for: patients who need Roger remote-microphone technology. Roger is the industry-leading wireless microphone system for schools, workplaces, meetings, restaurants with a partner across the table, and one-on-one distance listening. If Roger is a fit for your listening life, Phonak is not "one of the choices." It is the choice. The DEEPSONIC AI chip also applies more raw-audio deep learning to noise reduction than any competitor.

Not the top pick for: patients who do not need Roger, patients who want the longest rechargeable battery, or patients who want on-board fall detection.

Read the full audiologist review of Phonak Audeo Sphere Infinio.

ReSound Nexia

Best for: patients who care about Auracast and Bluetooth LE Audio breadth today. ReSound was the first flagship to ship broad Auracast reception, and their firmware rollout is ahead of the pack. If your listening life includes lecture halls, worship services with looped audio, airports, or public venues that are starting to install Auracast broadcasters, Nexia is the strongest current pick. Excellent iPhone MFi and Android ASHA streaming as well.

Not the top pick for: patients whose main need is multi-speaker group conversation (Signia wins), Roger connectivity (Phonak wins), or on-board fall detection (Starkey wins).

Read the full audiologist review of ReSound Nexia.

Starkey Genesis AI

Best for: patients who want on-board fall detection with texted alerts sent to up to three emergency contacts. Also the top pick for Live Language Translation (52 languages in the My Starkey app) and for the highest power ceiling in a rechargeable RIC (up to 105 dB, fitting severe-to-profound hearing loss without moving to a BTE form factor). The on-chip Neuro Processor and Edge Mode+ boost let patients manually push processing power in a hard noise moment.

Not the top pick for: patients whose main need is multi-speaker group tracking (Signia wins) or Roger connectivity (Phonak wins).

Read the full audiologist review of Starkey Genesis AI.

How to actually pick one

You do not have to pick the right platform on your own. That is our job at ACI. What we ask you to bring to the fitting:

  • A recent audiogram if you have one (we can also do one on the day)
  • A written list of the top 5 listening situations you struggle with, from worst to lightest
  • Whether you tried a hearing aid in the past that you returned, and if so, what specifically bothered you (own voice? feedback? sound quality? phone? something else?)
  • Your insurance card if you have hearing aid benefits
  • Any specific brand or technology you have already been recommended

With that information plus your audiogram we can narrow the five flagship platforms down to the one or two that fit your listening life best, do in-clinic listening demos on the day so you can hear the difference, and start a 60-day trial on the platform you pick. If it is not the right match, we refit you at no additional charge inside the trial window.
