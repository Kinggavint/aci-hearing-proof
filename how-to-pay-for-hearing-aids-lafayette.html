How to Pay for Hearing Aids in Lafayette: 6 Payment Paths at ACI

A working Doctor of Audiology walks through every way patients pay for hearing aid treatment plans at ACI Hearing Center in Lafayette Louisiana. Medicare Advantage. Louisiana Medicaid. VA benefits. Workers comp. HSA/FSA. Financing. Every path explained in plain English, with pricing anchors and next steps.

Written and reviewed by Kimberly L. Allred, Au.D., Doctor of Audiology, ACI Hearing Center, Lafayette LA. Last updated August 7, 2026.

The short version

There are six ways patients pay for hearing aid treatment plans at ACI Hearing Center. Most patients combine two or three of them. Our team runs a free benefits verification before your fitting appointment so you know exactly what your out-of-pocket cost will be.

  1. Medicare Advantage hearing aid benefit (allowance or network like TruHearing)
  2. Louisiana Medicaid for children under 21 and adults with medical necessity
  3. VA benefits for eligible veterans (no cost, full coverage)
  4. Louisiana workers compensation for workplace noise-induced hearing loss
  5. HSA/FSA funds as a qualified medical expense
  6. Direct pay with CareCredit or PowerPay financing (0% APR promotional terms available)

Treatment plan pricing at ACI runs $4,500 to $7,500 per pair. Includes evaluation, fitting, follow-ups for the life of the devices, batteries or charging accessories, and a 60-day trial. All prescription hearing aid brands we fit (Signia, Oticon, Phonak, ReSound, Starkey) fall in the same price range. You are paying for the technology tier, not the brand.

Path 1: Medicare Advantage

If you are 65 or older, or if you qualify for Medicare through disability, and you enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan (Part C) instead of staying on Original Medicare, your plan may include a hearing aid benefit. This is different from Original Medicare Part B, which does not cover hearing aids at all.

Louisiana Medicare Advantage plans typically deliver the hearing benefit one of two ways:

  • Allowance. Your plan gives you a set dollar amount to spend on hearing aids (usually $500 to $3,000 per ear, per benefit period). You pay the difference between the allowance and the treatment plan price.
  • Hearing benefit network. Your plan sends you to a specific network like TruHearing where you pay a fixed copay ($699 to $2,399 per device) for a limited menu of hearing aid models. ACI Hearing Center is a TruHearing provider.

The Louisiana Medicare Advantage plans we work with most often include Humana, Blue Cross Blue Shield of Louisiana, Peoples Health, Aetna, and UnitedHealthcare. Every plan is different. Read our Medicare Advantage hearing aid coverage in Louisiana guide for what your specific plan covers.

Path 2: Louisiana Medicaid

Louisiana Medicaid covers hearing aids for two groups:

  • Children under age 21. Full coverage under the EPSDT (Early and Periodic Screening, Diagnostic, and Treatment) benefit. This covers evaluation, hearing aids, follow-up, and replacement as clinically indicated.
  • Adults with documented medical necessity. Coverage requires prior authorization and medical necessity documentation. Not every adult on Louisiana Medicaid qualifies. Our team helps navigate the paperwork.

Louisiana Medicaid managed care organizations we work with include Aetna Better Health, AmeriHealth Caritas, Healthy Blue, Humana Healthy Horizons, and Louisiana Healthcare Connections. Note: UnitedHealthcare Community Plan's contract ended December 31, 2025, and members were reassigned to one of the five remaining plans. For the full breakdown of what each plan covers for adult hearing benefits, EPSDT documentation requirements, and prior authorization, see our Louisiana Medicaid hearing aid coverage guide. Path 3: VA benefits

Enrolled veterans get hearing aids at no cost through VA healthcare. The VA covers the full evaluation, hearing aids, batteries, and follow-up care. Coverage is broad and includes flagship-tier hearing aid platforms.

There are two ways Lafayette veterans access hearing care:

  • VA facility. The Alexandria VA (Fort Polk) audiology clinic is the closest VA hearing care location for most Lafayette veterans.
  • VA Community Care. If the VA cannot see you in a reasonable time or distance, the VA authorizes you to see a community audiologist like ACI. Coverage is the same at no cost to you.

Community Care authorization is not automatic. It has to be initiated through the VA. Our team walks eligible veterans through the process.

Path 4: Louisiana workers compensation

If your hearing loss is caused by workplace noise exposure and you have an accepted Louisiana workers compensation claim, workers comp covers the full cost of evaluation, hearing aids, and follow-up care. Common Acadiana industries that generate hearing loss workers comp claims: offshore oil and gas, industrial construction, manufacturing, transportation.

Louisiana law (LA R.S. 23:1031.1) treats noise-induced hearing loss as an occupational disease. Under LA Admin Code tit. 40, section I-3325, the workers comp carrier or self-insured employer pays for the hearing aid when the loss is documented by a physician or by an audiologist with a Certificate of Clinical Competence in Audiology (CCC-A). All four ACI audiologists hold the Au.D. and qualify to document your claim.

You have 1 year to file from the date you know the hearing loss is job-related. Federal maritime and longshore workers on the Gulf Coast may file under the LHWCA instead, and under the LHWCA you can choose your own audiologist.

Path 5: HSA and FSA funds

Hearing aids are a qualified medical expense under IRS rules. If you have an HSA (Health Savings Account) or FSA (Flexible Spending Account) through your employer, you can use those funds to pay for hearing aids, batteries, and audiologist fees.

Two things to know:

  • FSA funds are use-it-or-lose-it. Most FSAs zero out at the end of the plan year (usually December 31). If you have unused FSA money in Q4, hearing aids are one of the highest-value ways to spend it. Do not wait until December 30.
  • HSA funds roll over. Unlike an FSA, HSA money you do not use this year stays in your account and keeps growing. HSA funds can also be combined with insurance benefits or financing.

Read our HSA and FSA hearing aid guide for the full breakdown including which forms you need for reimbursement.

Path 6: Direct pay with CareCredit or PowerPay financing

Most patients who do not have insurance coverage (or whose coverage is limited) pay for their treatment plan directly and finance the balance. ACI works with two financing partners:

  • CareCredit. Healthcare-specific financing with promotional 0% APR terms (typically 12 to 24 months). Subject to credit approval.
  • PowerPay. Similar 0% APR promotional terms. Subject to credit approval.

Financing pairs well with HSA/FSA funds and can also be combined with a Medicare Advantage allowance to cover the balance above the allowance. ACI does not offer Cherry as a financing option.
