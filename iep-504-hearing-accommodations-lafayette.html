Why school hearing plans matter

Kids with hearing loss can succeed in regular classrooms. They just need the right supports. A written plan makes sure the teacher, the principal, and every substitute all know what your child needs. Without a written plan, accommodations tend to fade after a few weeks.

Two laws create these plans in Louisiana public schools. The first is IDEA, which stands for the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act. IDEA is a federal special education law. It creates the IEP, or Individualized Education Program. The second is Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act. Section 504 is a civil rights law. It creates the 504 plan.

Both plans are free. Both are written by the school with input from parents. Both are enforceable by law.

IEP vs 504 plan explained plainly

Think of it this way. An IEP is for kids who need special instruction because of hearing loss. That could be speech therapy at school, a teacher of the deaf and hard of hearing, or a smaller class for reading. A 504 plan is for kids who can learn in a regular classroom but need accommodations so they can hear and understand the lessons.

Most kids with mild, moderate, or unilateral hearing loss get a 504 plan. Most kids with severe or profound hearing loss get an IEP. The line is not exact. The school decides based on the evaluation and how much the hearing loss affects learning.

The good news: you do not have to pick. Ask the school for an evaluation and let the team recommend the right plan for your child. If you disagree, you can appeal.

How to get the process started in Lafayette Parish

Here is the exact sequence that works for LPSS families:

  1. Get a current audiology evaluation. The school needs a report that is less than one year old. ACI Hearing Center writes school-ready reports that include the audiogram, the type and degree of hearing loss, and a plain-English section on educational impact. Call (337) 223-9448 to book.
  2. Write the request letter. Address it to the school principal and to the LPSS Special Education Department. State clearly: "I am requesting an evaluation for special education services or a 504 plan for my child based on hearing loss." Attach the audiology report. Date it. Keep a copy.
  3. Sign the consent form. The school will send you a consent form. Once you sign it, Louisiana law gives the district 60 calendar days to finish the evaluation and hold the eligibility meeting.
  4. Attend the meeting. Bring the audiologist letter, past report cards, and any teacher notes about listening or attention. Ask questions. Ask the audiologist to attend if the hearing loss is complex.
  5. Review the plan every year. IEPs are reviewed at least once a year. 504 plans are reviewed at least once a year, more often if the hearing changes. Bring a fresh audiology report to each review.

Hearing accommodations by grade level

The right accommodations depend on your child's age, degree of hearing loss, and classroom setup. This list gives you a starting point.

Preschool and kindergarten

  • Personal FM or Roger remote microphone system worn by the teacher
  • Preferential seating close to the teacher
  • Visual signals for transitions
  • Daily equipment check by classroom aide
  • Speech and language support from a school speech-language pathologist

Elementary school

  • Personal FM or Roger system with a pass-around microphone for group discussion
  • Preferential seating, away from the door and windows
  • Captioning on all videos and morning announcements
  • Copy of teacher notes or peer note-taker
  • Testing in a quiet room with extended time
  • Visual fire alarm and drill signals

Middle and high school

  • Personal FM or Roger system across all class periods
  • Preferential seating in every classroom
  • Captioning on all instructional videos
  • Note-taking help, either peer notes or a laptop
  • Testing in a quiet room with extended time on standardized tests including the LEAP, ACT, and SAT
  • Access to a private space during lunch or between classes for hearing aid or cochlear implant care
  • Advance copies of PowerPoint slides for lecture-heavy classes

ACI can write the specific accommodations into the audiologist letter so the school team knows what to ask for.

What the audiologist letter looks like

A strong audiologist letter for an IEP or 504 meeting includes seven parts:

  1. Child's name, date of birth, and date of the evaluation
  2. Type and degree of hearing loss in each ear, with audiogram attached
  3. Speech recognition scores in quiet and in noise
  4. Current hearing technology, including hearing aid model, cochlear implant, or bone-anchored device
  5. Statement of educational impact in plain English
  6. Specific recommended accommodations with reasoning
  7. Contact information for the audiologist

ACI writes all seven parts into every school letter. If the school pushes back on an accommodation, we can attend the meeting to explain the reasoning. Call (337) 223-9448 at least two weeks before the meeting to schedule.

FM systems and Roger devices

An FM or Roger system is a small radio-style device. The teacher wears a microphone. The signal is sent directly into the child's hearing aids or cochlear implants. This cuts background noise and helps the child hear the teacher clearly from across the room. The American Academy of Audiology considers these systems evidence-based standard practice for children with hearing loss in classrooms.

Two things matter for coverage:

  • If the FM system is written into an IEP as needed for a Free Appropriate Public Education, the school district must provide it at no cost under IDEA.
  • If the child only has a 504 plan, the school is not required to buy the FM system. Many LPSS schools still do. If the school will not, Louisiana Medicaid EPSDT may cover a personal FM system when medically necessary. See our Louisiana Medicaid hearing aid coverage guide for how EPSDT works.

What if my child is under age 3?

Children under age 3 in Louisiana get services through EarlySteps, the state Part C early intervention program. EarlySteps writes an Individualized Family Service Plan (IFSP) instead of an IEP. The IFSP focuses on the family and can include home visits, speech therapy, hearing aid fitting, and parent training.

When your child turns 3, EarlySteps helps the family transition into the local school district for an IEP or 504 plan. ACI works with EarlySteps and Louisiana EHDI so the handoff is smooth. See our newborn hearing screening follow-up guide for the earlier stages.

Call EarlySteps at (866) 327-5978 to start Part C services, or call our office and we will help with the referral.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between an IEP and a 504 plan?

An IEP is a special education plan under IDEA. It is used when a child needs specialized instruction because of hearing loss, not just accommodations. A 504 plan is a civil rights accommodation plan under Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act. It is used when a child can learn in a regular classroom with accommodations like an FM system or preferential seating. Many children with hearing loss qualify for one or the other. A few qualify for both.

How do I request an IEP or 504 evaluation in Lafayette Parish?

Write a dated letter to the school principal and the LPSS Special Education Department. State that you are requesting an evaluation for special education services or a 504 plan based on hearing loss. Attach the most recent audiology report. Louisiana law gives the school 60 calendar days after your written consent to finish the evaluation and hold the eligibility meeting.

What hearing accommodations are common in an IEP or 504 plan?

Common accommodations include a personal FM or Roger remote microphone system worn by the teacher, preferential seating in front of the class and away from noise sources, captioning for videos and morning announcements, testing in a quiet room, extended time on tests, a copy of teacher notes or a note-taker, visual signals for fire drills, and a check-in with the audiologist or speech therapist.

Will insurance or Medicaid pay for the FM system?

If the FM system is written into an IEP as needed for a Free Appropriate Public Education, the school district is required to provide it under IDEA at no cost to the family. If the child only has a 504 plan, the school is not required to buy the FM system, but many districts still do. Louisiana Medicaid may cover a personal FM system through the EPSDT benefit when medically necessary.

Can my audiologist come to the school meeting?

Yes. On request, an ACI audiologist can attend the IEP or 504 meeting in person or by video call. Having the audiologist in the room helps the school understand the audiogram and pick the right accommodations. Call (337) 223-9448 at least two weeks before the meeting so we can put it on the schedule.

My child has mild or unilateral hearing loss. Does that still count?

Yes. Mild hearing loss and unilateral (one-sided) hearing loss both affect learning in noisy classrooms. Research shows children with unilateral hearing loss are 10 times more likely to be held back a grade. Ask the school for an evaluation.

What if my child is under age 3?

Children under 3 in Louisiana are served by EarlySteps, which is the state Part C early intervention program. EarlySteps writes an Individualized Family Service Plan (IFSP) instead of an IEP. When your child turns 3, EarlySteps transitions the child into the local school system for an IEP if needed. Call EarlySteps at (866) 327-5978 or ask ACI to help with the referral.
