What Noise-Induced Hearing Loss Is and Why Oilfield Workers Are at High Risk

Noise-induced hearing loss, or NIHL, is permanent hearing damage caused by long-term exposure to loud noise. It happens when the tiny hair cells inside the inner ear are worn down by sound levels above 85 decibels. Once those hair cells are damaged, they do not grow back. Hearing aids can help you understand speech again, but they cannot restore the original hearing.

Lafayette Parish and the surrounding Acadiana region sit at the center of Louisiana onshore and offshore oilfield services. Iberia, Vermilion, Lafourche, and Terrebonne parishes have some of the highest concentrations of oilfield and offshore workers in the United States. These jobs put your ears in the highest-risk category for permanent hearing damage. Typical noise levels on the job include:

  • Drilling floor: 100 to 115 decibels during rotation
  • Helideck operations: 110 to 120 decibels
  • Refinery process units: 90 to 110 decibels
  • Compressor stations: 95 to 105 decibels
  • Welding, grinding, and cutting: 100 to 115 decibels
  • Impact tools and hydraulic breakers: 110 to 130 decibels

OSHA requires employers to protect workers when noise exposure reaches or exceeds 85 decibels averaged over an 8-hour shift. Many oilfield tasks blow past that threshold in minutes.

Signs You May Have Occupational Hearing Loss

NIHL usually shows up gradually, often in the higher frequencies first. That means people with early NIHL still hear most speech but miss the crisp consonant sounds that carry meaning. Common early signs include:

  • Trouble understanding speech in a noisy break room or truck cab
  • Missing the s, sh, f, and th sounds so words like sit, ship, fit, and thin start to blend
  • Constant ringing, buzzing, or humming in one or both ears (tinnitus)
  • Family members saying the TV is too loud
  • Feeling like your ears are plugged or muffled at the end of a shift
  • Trouble hearing radio calls, alarms, or high-pitched warning tones

If any of these sound familiar, get evaluated. The earlier a threshold shift is documented, the stronger any future workers-comp claim will be.

What a Louisiana Workers-Comp Hearing Evaluation Looks Like at ACI

Every workers-compensation hearing evaluation at ACI Hearing Center follows the same structure. It typically takes 45 to 60 minutes and includes:

  1. Otoscopy: a licensed audiologist looks in each ear canal to check the eardrum and rule out wax blockage or infection.
  2. Pure-tone audiogram: measures the softest sound you can hear at each frequency from 250 to 8000 hertz, in each ear separately.
  3. Tympanometry: checks middle ear pressure and eardrum movement so we can separate conductive from sensorineural loss.
  4. Speech-in-noise testing: measures how well you understand words with background noise, which matches what you experience on a rig or in a plant.
  5. Threshold-shift comparison: if your employer has any prior baseline audiograms on file, we compare current thresholds against them to document any standard threshold shift (STS) or occupational threshold shift.
  6. Documentation: we complete the required Louisiana Office of Workers Compensation Administration paperwork and provide a physician clearance letter when needed.

What Louisiana Workers Compensation Covers

Louisiana workers compensation covers medical care that is reasonable and necessary to treat a work-related injury. For documented occupational hearing loss, that typically includes:

  • The initial audiologic evaluation
  • Prescription hearing aids programmed to your hearing loss
  • Real Ear Measurement verification (the audiology standard of care)
  • Follow-up appointments to fine-tune the fitting
  • Hearing aid batteries or charging accessories
  • Replacement hearing aids on a documented schedule, usually every 5 to 6 years
  • Tinnitus management when work-related tinnitus is documented alongside NIHL

Coverage details depend on your employer, carrier, and the specifics of your claim. Call 337-223-9448 with your employer name and carrier and we will verify coverage before your appointment.

Hearing Protection That Lets You Keep Working

Foam plugs alone are not enough for most oilfield tasks. They over-attenuate, so you cannot hear equipment warnings, radio calls, or a coworker shouting from 10 feet away. That trade-off leads workers to pull the plugs out, which defeats the purpose. ACI fits custom electronic hearing protection that blocks damaging noise while keeping speech and warning sounds audible. The products we most often fit for oilfield and offshore workers include:

  • 3M PELTOR electronic muffs: over-the-ear active hearing protection with talk-through mics, popular on drilling rigs and helipads.
  • Etymotic MusicPro electronic earplugs: in-ear active plugs that automatically dial back sound above safe levels while letting normal speech pass through.
  • Westone DefendEar Digital: custom-molded electronic plugs with adjustable programs for shooting range, industrial, and communication use.
  • Custom-molded passive plugs: for workers who need a maximum-attenuation option without electronics.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Louisiana workers comp pay for hearing tests and hearing aids for oilfield workers?

Yes. Louisiana workers compensation covers audiologic evaluations, hearing aids, and follow-up care when hearing loss is documented as work-related. ACI Hearing Center completes the required Office of Workers Compensation Administration paperwork and coordinates with your employer carrier.

How loud is drilling floor and offshore platform noise?

Drilling floor noise commonly measures 100 to 115 decibels during operations. Helideck operations can reach 110 to 120 decibels. Refinery process areas typically run 90 to 110 decibels. Any sustained exposure above 85 decibels can cause permanent hearing damage without proper hearing protection.

What does a workers comp hearing evaluation include at ACI?

Every evaluation includes an audiogram measuring hearing thresholds at each frequency, otoscopy to examine the ear canal and eardrum, tympanometry to check middle ear function, speech-in-noise testing, and threshold-shift analysis compared to any prior baseline audiograms your employer has on file.

What hearing protection does ACI recommend for active oilfield and offshore workers?

ACI fits custom electronic hearing protection that lets you hear conversations and equipment warnings while blocking damaging noise levels. Common products include 3M PELTOR electronic muffs, Westone DefendEar Digital, and Etymotic MusicPro filtered plugs. Custom-molded plugs give the best seal for high-noise environments.

Do I need a baseline audiogram before I start an oilfield job?

Yes. OSHA requires employers with hazardous noise exposure to obtain a baseline audiogram within six months of hire. This baseline is what future threshold-shift claims are measured against. If your employer never obtained one or you cannot find it, ACI can still document current hearing status and work with your carrier.

Which oilfield employers does ACI Hearing Center work with?

ACI Hearing Center works with all major Acadiana oilfield services companies through their workers compensation insurance carriers. Call 337-223-9448 with your employer name and carrier so we can verify coverage and schedule your evaluation.
