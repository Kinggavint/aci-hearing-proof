What is a pediatric hearing aid?

A pediatric hearing aid is a hearing aid that carries an FDA clearance for use in children. It has three things a standard adult hearing aid does not have:

  • A locking battery door so a young child cannot open the battery compartment.
  • A tamper-resistant earmold with pediatric retention, sized to a small ear canal.
  • Color options and pediatric styling so kids feel proud to wear them.

Most pediatric hearing aids also carry a loss and damage warranty for the first two to three years, which is a real thing because kids are kids. On top of that, every current pediatric hearing aid pairs with a classroom FM or DM system so the teacher's voice can be streamed directly to the aids.

Pediatric fitting is also different from adult fitting. Small ear canals mean the sound level inside the ear is not the same as the sound level a hearing aid puts out on a test box. That is why the American Academy of Audiology Clinical Practice Guidelines for Pediatric Amplification require real-ear measurement, or REM, for every pediatric fitting. Every ACI pediatric fitting includes REM verification.

Pediatric fitting by age

Infants (birth to 12 months)

Louisiana requires a newborn hearing screening at every birth. If the screening does not pass, follow-up testing at ACI or an ABR clinic confirms whether the baby has hearing loss and how much. If hearing aids are needed, most infants are fit by 3 to 6 months of age. Infant fittings use very soft pediatric earmolds and rely on family-report data plus objective ABR to set the gain. Earmolds need to be remade every 4 to 8 weeks in the first year as the ear grows.

Toddlers and preschool (1 to 5 years)

Toddlers benefit from durable pediatric aids with lock-out features on the volume and battery door. Colored earmolds are usually the norm. This is also when early intervention programs like EarlySteps in Louisiana come into play. We coordinate directly with the EarlySteps team so the child's amplification is set up before speech and language milestones fall behind.

School age (6 to 12 years)

School-age kids need classroom access, not just close-conversation hearing. This is when the FM or DM system enters the picture. ACI writes the audiology report the school needs, coordinates with the teacher of the deaf, and makes sure the pediatric aids pair correctly with the classroom system. We also handle IEP and 504 plan documentation.

Teens (13 to 20)

Teens want small, cosmetic devices they can stream music from directly. Most current pediatric aids look almost identical to adult aids. Louisiana Medicaid EPSDT still covers hearing aids at no cost up through age 20, so teens who qualify keep the full benefit until their 21st birthday.

Cost and coverage for pediatric hearing aids in Louisiana

Louisiana has three main payment paths for pediatric hearing aids:

Louisiana Medicaid EPSDT (children under 21)

Family cost: zero. This is the strongest pediatric benefit in the state. EPSDT is a federal Medicaid rule that requires every state to cover hearing aids for enrolled children at no cost. Coverage includes:

  • The hearing aids themselves.
  • Earmolds and replacement earmolds as the child grows.
  • Batteries.
  • Repairs (if the hearing aid was originally paid for by Medicaid).
  • The two-year warranty required by the state DME manual.

Prior authorization is required and the DME provider handles it. See our full Louisiana Medicaid hearing aid coverage guide for the paperwork and timeline.

Private insurance (children under 18)

Louisiana Revised Statute 22:1038 requires state-regulated insurers, HMOs, and the Office of Group Benefits to cover hearing aids for children under 18. The benefit is limited to $1,400 per hearing aid per ear every 36 months and requires physician clearance and a fitting by a licensed audiologist. If your plan is self-funded through a large employer, Louisiana state law may not apply. Call the number on your card and ask two things: is my plan fully insured or self-funded, and does it cover a pediatric hearing aid benefit?

Self-pay

A complete pediatric fitting including the aids, custom earmolds, real-ear verification, and one year of follow-up runs $4,500 to $7,500 for the pair. We offer CareCredit and PowerPay financing to help families spread the cost. We do not use Cherry.

See our full insurance and payment options page for how all these paths fit together.

School coordination, IEPs, and 504 plans

Federal IDEA law (Individuals with Disabilities Education Act) gives every child with hearing loss the right to a Free Appropriate Public Education. In practice, that often means:

  • A classroom FM or DM system paired with the child's hearing aids.
  • Preferential seating near the teacher.
  • Written accommodations under an IEP or a 504 plan.
  • Coordination with a teacher of the deaf.

ACI Hearing Center writes the audiology report the school district needs, verifies the pediatric hearing aid pairs correctly with the classroom system, and joins IEP meetings when the family asks us to. Lafayette Parish School System, Vermilion Parish, Iberia Parish, and St. Landry Parish all recognize private-audiology reports for IEP eligibility.

What a pediatric evaluation at ACI looks like

  1. Full history and family interview. Newborn hearing screening result if available. Speech and language milestones. School concerns.
  2. Age-appropriate audiologic testing. Otoacoustic emissions, tympanometry, behavioral audiometry, visual reinforcement audiometry, or conditioned play audiometry depending on age. ABR referral if needed.
  3. Diagnosis and family counseling. Type, degree, and configuration of the hearing loss, plus what it means for daily life and school.
  4. Hearing aid selection. Match the audiogram, age, activity level, and family budget to the right pediatric device.
  5. Earmold impressions and pediatric fitting. Custom earmolds in colors the child can pick.
  6. Real-ear measurement verification. Every pediatric fitting is verified with REM adapted for small ear canals. This is a non-negotiable pediatric standard.
  7. Follow-up care. New earmolds as the child grows, gain adjustments, school report updates, and yearly re-evaluations. Ongoing troubleshooting anytime you need it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What age can a child get hearing aids?

Babies can be fit with hearing aids as young as a few weeks old. If a newborn hearing screening in the hospital comes back with a possible hearing loss, follow-up testing is done in the first few weeks of life. Most children who need hearing aids are fit by 3 to 6 months of age. Early fitting matters because the brain builds its speech and language pathways in the first three years.

Does Louisiana Medicaid cover hearing aids for kids?

Yes. Louisiana Medicaid covers hearing aids at no cost to the family for every child under 21 through the EPSDT benefit. This is a federal Medicaid rule that every state must follow. Coverage includes the hearing aids, earmolds, batteries, and repairs. A prior authorization is required and the DME provider handles it. See our full Louisiana Medicaid guide for the details.

Will my child lose the hearing aids?

This is the number one parent worry and it is a real one. Pediatric hearing aids have several built-in safety features: locking battery doors, tamper-resistant earmolds with pediatric retention, colored options that kids like wearing, and retention clips that attach to clothing. Loss and damage warranties are standard. We show you exactly how each device is designed to stay on and where insurance sits if something happens.

Will my child still be able to hear in class with hearing aids?

In many classrooms, hearing aids alone are not enough because of distance and background noise. That is where FM and DM systems come in. The teacher wears a small microphone and the signal goes directly to your child's hearing aids. Every major pediatric hearing aid is compatible with a school FM or DM system. ACI works with your child's school and the teacher of the deaf to set this up as part of an IEP or 504 plan.

How much do pediatric hearing aids cost?

For Louisiana Medicaid families with a child under 21, the family cost is zero. For families with private insurance, Louisiana state law requires state-regulated plans to cover at least $1,400 per hearing aid per ear every 36 months for children under 18. If your plan is self-funded through an employer, Louisiana state law may not apply and coverage varies. For self-pay, a full pediatric fitting including the aids, earmolds, verification, and follow-up runs $4,500 to $7,500 for the pair. We offer CareCredit and PowerPay financing options.

Which hearing aid brands work best for kids?

The main pediatric-cleared families are Oticon Play PX, Phonak Sky Lumity and Sky Marvel, ReSound Up Smart, Widex pediatric line, Signia and Bernafon pediatric models, and Starkey pediatric devices. The best brand depends on your child's hearing loss, age, activity level, and school setup. We fit every major pediatric brand and match the device to the child, not the other way around.

Do you work with schools on IEPs and 504 plans?

Yes. Under federal IDEA law, children with hearing loss have a right to a Free Appropriate Public Education, which can include FM or DM classroom systems, IEP accommodations, and 504 plans. ACI coordinates directly with your child's school, the teacher of the deaf, and the district audiologist to make sure the classroom setup matches the hearing aid fitting. We write the audiology reports the school needs.

How often do pediatric hearing aids need to be replaced?

The hearing aids themselves usually last 3 to 5 years. Louisiana Medicaid requires a two-year warranty and expects the aids to last at least three years before replacement. Earmolds are different: young children outgrow earmolds quickly, often every 3 to 6 months in the first years. Medicaid and most insurance plans cover replacement earmolds as part of the pediatric benefit.
