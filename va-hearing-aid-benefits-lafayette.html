VA hearing aid benefits for veterans in Lafayette Louisiana

Enrolled veterans get hearing aids at no cost through the VA. There are two paths, and ACI Hearing Center is a VA Community Care provider for Lafayette-area veterans. This guide explains both paths and how to start.

Free eligibility check: 337-223-9448

The short version

If you are an enrolled veteran, the VA pays for your hearing test, your hearing aids, batteries, repairs, and replacements. You pay nothing. You can get care in two ways:

  1. Alexandria VA Health Care System (the primary VA facility for most Lafayette-area veterans, about 90 miles and 90 minutes north on I-49). Audiology services are provided directly by VA staff.
  2. VA Community Care. If the VA cannot see you within the MISSION Act standards (30 days for a routine appointment or an average 30-minute drive time from your home), you may qualify for care at a private community provider. ACI Hearing Center in Lafayette accepts VA Community Care authorizations.

Both paths give you the same benefit. What differs is where you go and how long you wait.

Who qualifies

You qualify for VA hearing benefits if you are enrolled in VA healthcare. Enrollment is based on your service history and priority group assignment. The VA groups veterans into 8 priority groups based on factors like service-connected disability rating, income, and Purple Heart or Medal of Honor status.

Most honorably discharged veterans qualify for some level of enrollment. Some priority groups have small co-pays for certain services, but hearing aid coverage itself is comprehensive across all groups once you are enrolled. If you have never enrolled, apply at va.gov/health-care/apply or call 1-877-222-8387. You will need your DD-214 and Social Security number. Enrollment is free.

If you served in a combat zone, if you have a service-connected disability rating (any percentage), or if you are a former POW, Purple Heart recipient, or Medal of Honor recipient, you are almost certainly eligible. Do not assume you do not qualify. Apply first.

Path 1: Alexandria VA Health Care System

The Alexandria VA Health Care System is the primary VA facility for most veterans in the Acadiana region. It offers audiology services including diagnostic hearing evaluations, hearing aid fittings, and follow-up care.

Address: 2495 Shreveport Highway, Pineville, LA 71360
Main number: 318-473-0010
Audiology and Speech Pathology: Reachable through the main number or through your primary care team.

Drive time from Lafayette: about 90 miles and 90 to 100 minutes north on I-49.

To use this path, call your primary care VA team and ask to be scheduled for a hearing evaluation. Wait times vary by season and by the audiology clinic's current backlog. If the wait exceeds the MISSION Act 30-day standard, you can request VA Community Care instead.

Some Lafayette-area veterans also use the Alexandria VA Community-Based Outpatient Clinic (CBOC) at 2100 Jackson Street, Alexandria, LA. CBOCs generally do not have full audiology services, but they can begin the referral. Confirm with your VA team.

Path 2: VA Community Care through ACI Hearing Center

VA Community Care allows an enrolled veteran to receive care from a private community provider when specific criteria are met. This program was created under the VA MISSION Act of 2018 to reduce travel and wait times for veterans who cannot easily reach a VA facility.

When you qualify for Community Care

You qualify when at least one of these is true:

  • The VA cannot schedule you within 30 days of the clinically indicated date (or your preferred date, if later).
  • The average drive time from your home to the nearest VA facility offering the service exceeds 30 minutes for primary care and mental health, or 60 minutes for specialty care like audiology. For most Lafayette-area veterans, the drive to Alexandria exceeds this standard for audiology.
  • A VA clinician determines that community care is in your best medical interest.
  • The specific care you need is not offered at any VA facility within a reasonable distance.

How the authorization works

You start by calling the VA and requesting Community Care. Name ACI Hearing Center as your preferred community provider (address: 103 Saint Thomas Street, Lafayette, LA 70506; phone: 337-223-9448). The VA reviews your eligibility, confirms medical necessity, and issues a written authorization to ACI. You do not pay anything. The VA pays ACI directly for the covered services.

Typical timeline: 5 to 15 business days from your initial request to the authorization landing at ACI. The authorization is usually good for 6 months and covers a defined set of visits: evaluation, fitting, and follow-up. If the authorization expires or you need additional services beyond it, the VA can extend or reissue it.

What happens at your ACI appointment

Once the authorization is on file, we call you to schedule. Your first visit is a full diagnostic hearing evaluation. If you qualify for hearing aids based on the evaluation, we select devices from the VA formulary that fit your hearing loss pattern and your lifestyle. Fittings happen at a follow-up appointment. Adjustments continue over several weeks as your ears adapt to the devices.

Batteries, chargers, cleaning kits, and follow-up adjustments are all covered under the authorization. You pay nothing. If you have questions about the process, call us at 337-223-9448 and we will walk through it before you contact the VA.

What the VA hearing benefit covers

The benefit is comprehensive. It includes:

  • Diagnostic hearing evaluation
  • Hearing aids (one or both ears, based on medical need)
  • Hearing aid fitting and programming
  • Follow-up visits and adjustments for the life of the device
  • Batteries, chargers, and cleaning supplies
  • Repairs
  • Standard replacement every 4 to 5 years, or sooner if the device becomes unserviceable

The VA formulary includes all 5 flagship hearing aid platforms (Oticon Real, Phonak Audeo Sphere Infinio, ReSound Nexia, Starkey Genesis AI, Signia Integrated Xperience) plus a range of mid-tier options from Sonic, Rexton, Widex, and Unitron. Your audiologist recommends specific devices based on your hearing loss pattern and lifestyle.

Tinnitus care is also covered

Many veterans present with combined hearing loss and tinnitus, often connected to service-related noise exposure. Tinnitus is the number one service-connected disability among post-9/11 veterans. VA benefits cover tinnitus evaluation, sound therapy, and hearing aids with built-in tinnitus masking features.

If tinnitus is your primary concern, ask your VA audiology team about the Progressive Tinnitus Management protocol. It is a stepped care approach used across the VA system. Learn more on our tinnitus management page.

How to start

If you are not yet enrolled in VA healthcare, apply at va.gov/health-care/apply or call 1-877-222-8387. Bring your DD-214 and Social Security number.

If you are already enrolled and want to be seen for hearing:

  1. Call your primary VA team (usually the Alexandria VA at 318-473-0010) and request an audiology evaluation.
  2. If the wait exceeds 30 days, or if the drive time is too far, ask about VA Community Care.
  3. Name ACI Hearing Center in Lafayette as your preferred community provider.
  4. Wait for the authorization to reach ACI (typically 5 to 15 business days).
  5. Call ACI at 337-223-9448 to schedule once the authorization is on file, or call us first and we will help you understand the process.

If you are not sure whether you qualify, or if you have already been denied, call our office. We regularly work with Lafayette-area veterans and can talk through the process before you spend any time on paperwork.
