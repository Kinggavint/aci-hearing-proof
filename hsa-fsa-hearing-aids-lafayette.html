HSA and FSA for hearing aids in Lafayette, LA

The IRS says hearing aids are a qualified medical expense. Your FSA has a use-it-or-lose-it deadline in December. Your HSA does not. Here is how to use both at ACI Hearing Center.

Reviewed by Kimberly L. Allred, Au.D., Doctor of Audiology. Last updated August 4, 2026.

Schedule your fitting: 337-223-9448 Book online

The 60-second version

  • Hearing aids are IRS-qualified. Publication 502 lists hearing aids, batteries, repairs, hearing tests, and tinnitus treatment as qualified medical expenses.
  • FSA deadline: December 31 for most plans. Some have a March 15 grace period or a $640 carryover. Most have neither.
  • HSA deadline: none. Funds roll over year after year, portable between employers, no expiration.
  • Fitting timeline: 60 to 90 days from initial consult to final device.
  • If you have FSA funds and hearing loss, call us in September or October. Not December. There is not enough time in December to complete the trial before the deadline.

Every year we hear from patients in mid-December realizing they have $2,000 to $5,000 in unused FSA. That money goes back to their employer on January 1. Do not be one of those patients.

What HSA and FSA cover for hearing care

Every item and service in this list is qualified under IRS Publication 502. That means HSA and FSA funds pay for all of it, tax-free.

  • Hearing aids (all technology tiers)
  • Hearing aid batteries (zinc-air 10, 13, 312, 675)
  • Rechargeable charging cases
  • Hearing aid repairs
  • Receiver replacements
  • Wax guards and dome tips
  • Custom earmolds
  • Hearing tests and evaluations
  • Follow-up adjustment visits
  • Real-ear measurement fittings
  • Tinnitus treatments (Lenire, Neuromonics, sound therapy)
  • Cochlear implant devices and processors
  • Cochlear implant batteries and accessories
  • Assistive listening devices (Roger microphones, TV streamers)
  • Custom hearing protection
  • Ear cleaning and earwax removal
  • Assistive alerting devices (visual smoke alarms, bed shakers)
  • Hearing aid dehumidifiers and dry-and-store systems

The December 31 problem

FSAs run on a plan year. Most calendar-year FSAs end December 31. Whatever balance is left goes back to the employer. There are three possible relief valves, and most plans have none of them.

  1. March 15 grace period. Some plans give you until March 15 of the following year to spend down the prior year's balance. You cannot roll it forward, only backward.
  2. $640 carryover (2026 IRS limit). Some plans let you carry up to $640 of unused FSA into the next plan year. Anything above that is forfeited.
  3. Both. Some plans offer both grace period AND carryover.

Louisiana state employees on the state group FSA typically have neither. LSU and University of Louisiana Lafayette faculty and staff usually have the grace period. Lafayette Parish School System varies by contract. LHC Group employees, Stuller staff, Ochsner Lafayette General staff, and other private-employer plans: check your plan document or call HR. If you are not sure, we can help you look it up during the consultation.

Now the math. Say you have $3,000 in FSA funds and you contribute $150 per paycheck. If you do not spend that $3,000 by December 31, it is gone. That is $3,000 you already paid into the account with pre-tax dollars. If your marginal tax rate is 22 percent, that $3,000 in FSA is equivalent to about $3,850 of pre-tax salary. Losing it is the same as losing $3,850 of income.

The fitting timeline (why September and October matter)

Hearing aid treatment at ACI Hearing Center follows a standard timeline. Here is the calendar view.

  1. Week 1: Initial consult. Discussion of listening priorities, medical history, review of any prior audiograms. About 45 minutes.
  2. Week 1 or 2: Comprehensive hearing test. Air conduction, bone conduction, word recognition testing, tinnitus workup if indicated. About 60 minutes.
  3. Week 2: Device selection. Trial recommendations based on your audiogram and listening life. We show you the actual devices.
  4. Week 4 or 5: Fitting day. Devices arrive from the manufacturer 2 to 3 weeks after order. Custom fit, real-ear measurement verification, initial programming. About 90 minutes.
  5. Week 6: Follow-up 1. First fine-tune based on real-world use.
  6. Week 8: Follow-up 2. Second fine-tune.
  7. Week 11: Follow-up 3. Final fine-tune before the trial ends.
  8. Week 12: Trial decision day (60 days after fitting). Keep, return, or exchange.

Total: 12 weeks from initial consult to trial decision. Some patients finish faster if they already have a recent audiogram or if the device comes in stock. Some patients take longer if they exchange during the trial.

Now map that to the FSA deadline. If your FSA runs out December 31 and you want a keep decision before the deadline, count backward 12 weeks: October 7. If you want to be safe with some margin, count backward 14 weeks: September 23. That is why September and October are the right time to schedule if you have FSA funds.

The HSA plays different

HSAs are a different animal. Here is what HSA holders should know:

  • No expiration. Funds you contributed 5 or 10 years ago are still available.
  • Portable. If you change employers, your HSA comes with you.
  • Triple tax advantage. Contributions are pre-tax (or tax-deductible), growth is tax-free, and withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax-free.
  • Investment growth. Many HSA custodians let you invest the balance in mutual funds once it exceeds a threshold. A patient who has been saving for 10 years may have significantly more than what they contributed.
  • Retirement flexibility. After age 65, you can withdraw HSA funds for non-medical expenses and pay only regular income tax (no penalty). But for hearing aids specifically, you are always better using HSA funds because it is tax-free.

The practical implication at ACI: HSA holders who have been saving for several years often have $10,000+ available. That covers a Premium tier Sphere Infinio 90 or Oticon Real 1 fitting in full with no financing needed.

Combining HSA/FSA with CareCredit or PowerPay

Most patients have partial HSA or FSA funds and need to cover the rest another way. This is a common ACI conversation. The math looks like this:

Situation HSA/FSA Financed Result
$6,000 treatment, $2,500 FSA $2,500 FSA at fitting $3,500 on CareCredit 12mo interest-free $292/mo, zero FSA loss
$5,500 treatment, $4,000 HSA $4,000 HSA at fitting $1,500 on PowerPay 24mo $63/mo, HSA preserved for other care
$7,500 Premium tier, $1,500 FSA $1,500 FSA at fitting $6,000 on CareCredit 24mo $250/mo, small FSA maximized
$4,500 Essential tier, $6,000 HSA $4,500 HSA at fitting $0 financed Zero out-of-pocket, $1,500 HSA preserved

Louisiana employer plans we see often

These are the plans we see most at ACI Hearing Center in Lafayette. Confirm your specific plan details with your HR department or plan document, because plans change from year to year.

  • Louisiana Office of Group Benefits (state employees): FSA typically December 31 hard deadline, no grace period.
  • LSU / LSU Health Shreveport / LSU Health New Orleans: FSA typically has March 15 grace period.
  • University of Louisiana at Lafayette (UL Lafayette): FSA typically has grace period.
  • Lafayette Parish School System: Varies by contract year.
  • Ochsner Lafayette General / Our Lady of Lourdes: Employer plan, check with HR.
  • Stuller Inc: Employer plan, check with HR.
  • LHC Group: Employer plan, check with HR.
  • Federal employees (VA hospital, DoD, USPS): Federal FSAFEDS typically has $640 carryover.
  • Medicare / Medicaid patients: Typically no HSA/FSA. Some Medicare Advantage plans have flex allowances. See our Medicare Advantage hearing aid coverage page.
