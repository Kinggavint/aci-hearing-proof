Why your workers comp hearing loss claim needs a Doctor of Audiology

Louisiana law is clear about occupational hearing loss. In Arrant v. Graphic Packaging International, the Louisiana Supreme Court confirmed that noise-induced hearing loss is a covered occupational disease under the Louisiana Workers Compensation Act. That means medical treatment, hearing aids, and wage benefits can be part of your claim.

But the claim only works if your hearing loss is properly documented. Under Louisiana Admin Code Title 40 section I-3325, a hearing aid for a compensable claim must be prescribed by an ENT physician or a physician holding a Certificate of Clinical Competence in Audiology. That is a formal credential from the American Speech-Language-Hearing Association, and every Doctor of Audiology at ACI Hearing Center meets that standard.

An audiogram from a general clinic or an over-the-counter hearing test is not enough. Louisiana workers compensation adjusters, carriers, and administrative law judges expect a full audiologic workup from a licensed provider, with objective test data, threshold measurements, speech recognition scores, and a written report tying the pattern of loss to occupational noise exposure.

What a workers comp hearing evaluation at ACI includes

  • Full case history. Job title, industry, years of exposure, hearing protection use, prior audiograms if available, medical history, and family history of hearing loss.
  • Otoscopic examination. Direct visual check for wax, drum abnormalities, and other ear conditions that could confound results.
  • Immittance testing. Tympanometry and acoustic reflex testing to verify middle-ear function.
  • Pure-tone audiometry. Air and bone conduction thresholds at all frequencies used by the AMA Guides and by the Louisiana workers compensation reimbursement schedule.
  • Speech audiometry. Speech reception threshold and word recognition scores in quiet, and in noise when relevant.
  • Otoacoustic emissions. When needed to distinguish sensory from neural loss and to detect early cochlear damage that pure-tone audiometry can miss.
  • Written medical-legal report. Threshold table, audiogram, plain-English interpretation, and a professional opinion about the relationship between the pattern of loss and reported noise exposure.
  • AMA Guides impairment rating on request. Binaural hearing impairment percentage per the AMA Guides to the Evaluation of Permanent Impairment, 5th or 6th edition, as required by the claim.

Louisiana industries where we see occupational hearing loss

Lafayette and the Acadiana region have a heavy noise-exposure workforce. We regularly document hearing loss for workers from:

  • Oil and gas. Drilling floor, roustabouts, offshore platform workers, refinery operators, and production plant staff.
  • Chemical and petrochemical. Plant operators, maintenance mechanics, and turnaround contract workers.
  • Manufacturing and industrial. Metal fabrication, saw operators, press operators, and grinder operators.
  • Construction and heavy equipment. Pile drivers, jackhammer operators, road crews, and heavy equipment mechanics.
  • Maritime, shipyard, and longshore. Gulf Coast maritime workers may fall under the federal LHWCA rather than state workers comp. Different rules apply, including the worker's right to choose their own audiologist per Huntington Ingalls (5th Cir. 2023).
  • Firefighters and first responders. Sirens, apparatus noise, and blast exposure can produce compensable loss.
  • Agriculture. Sugar mill workers, rice mill workers, and equipment operators with sustained noise exposure.

How to start a workers comp hearing loss claim in Louisiana

  1. Report the loss to your employer in writing. Louisiana requires the employer to notify the Office of Workers Compensation within 10 days of learning about the injury.
  2. Ask for a workers comp claim form. Your employer or its carrier must provide the paperwork. Keep copies of everything you sign.
  3. Get an audiogram from a Doctor of Audiology or an ENT. Louisiana law requires this. A general hearing test from a big-box retailer or a screening at your primary care doctor does not meet the standard.
  4. Document your exposure. Job history, work sites, hearing protection issued, and noise measurements from the employer if available.
  5. Know your deadline. The Louisiana prescriptive period is one year, but the clock only starts once your hearing loss has manifested, disables you, and you know it is job related. Talk to a Louisiana workers comp attorney to confirm your deadline.
  6. Route the case to OWC District 4 if disputed. Located at 556 Jefferson Street on the first floor in Lafayette, phone 337-262-1057. Judge Sharon Morrow, mediator Dianne Mayo.

Frequently asked questions

Does Louisiana workers compensation cover hearing loss?

Yes. Louisiana law treats occupational noise-induced hearing loss as a covered occupational disease under the Louisiana Workers Compensation Act. The Louisiana Supreme Court confirmed this in Arrant v. Graphic Packaging International. If your hearing loss is work related, you may be entitled to medical treatment, hearing aids, and indemnity benefits under LA R.S. 23:1031.1.

What benefits can I get for a hearing loss claim in Louisiana?

Under Louisiana workers compensation, an employee with documented occupational hearing loss may receive coverage for audiometric examinations and hearing aids, up to 100 weeks of compensation for permanent hearing loss at 66 and two-thirds percent of wages, and up to 104 weeks of Supplemental Earnings Benefits if the loss prevents earning 90 percent of pre-injury wages. Federal longshore and shipyard workers on the Gulf Coast may qualify for benefits under the LHWCA instead.

How long do I have to file a hearing loss workers comp claim?

The prescriptive period is one year, but the one-year clock does not start until three conditions are met: the hearing loss has manifested, you are disabled by it, and you know or have reason to know it is job related. Because these conditions can be met years after the noise exposure ended, many claims stay viable long after leaving the job.

Do I need an ENT or can an audiologist evaluate me?

Under Louisiana Admin Code Title 40 section I-3325, hearing aids for compensable claims must be prescribed by either an ENT or a physician possessing a Certificate of Clinical Competence in Audiology (CCCA). All four Doctors of Audiology at ACI Hearing Center meet the CCCA standard. Audiometric examinations are a covered expense whether performed by a physician or an audiologist.

What is the Office of Workers Compensation District 4?

The Louisiana Office of Workers Compensation District 4 handles workers compensation disputes for Lafayette and the surrounding parishes. It is located at 556 Jefferson Street on the first floor in Lafayette, Louisiana, 70501. The phone number is 337-262-1057. Judge Sharon Morrow presides over District 4, with Dianne Mayo as mediator.

How much does a workers comp hearing evaluation cost?

If your claim is accepted by your employer or its workers compensation carrier, the audiogram, evaluation, and medical-legal report are covered under Louisiana Admin Code Title 40 section I-3325. If the claim is disputed or if you are pursuing a personal-pay Independent Medical Examination, ACI Hearing Center will provide a quote in advance. Call 337-223-9448 for current fee information.

Can I choose my own audiologist for the claim?

For most Louisiana workers compensation claims, the employer or its insurer may designate the treating provider, though the injured worker generally has the right to a second opinion. Federal longshore and shipyard workers covered by the LHWCA have the right to choose their own audiologist under the Fifth Circuit ruling in Huntington Ingalls v. Director OWCP (2023). ACI Hearing Center is available for both first-choice evaluations and second opinions.
